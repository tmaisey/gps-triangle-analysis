True for both pilots — a weak discriminator. Lift is found away from the course: for both pilots the climb rate roughly doubles from ~0.66 m/s on the line to ~1.0–1.2 m/s at 150–300 m out. The curves sit almost on top of each other, so this is a general soaring truth, not a Bill-vs-winner lever. The only edge is reach: the leader banks more total height far out (leader gained 18,706 m of climb over the rounds vs Bill's 15,490 m, and keeps climbing out at 300–500 m where Bill rarely ventures) — but that is really the tail story from chart 1, seen again.

Each point is a distance band; height plotted is metres gained per second spent flying in that band (positive altitude deltas only), pooled over the 14 distance rounds. Bands with under 60 s of pooled time are omitted. This measures where climbing actually happens, not merely where pilots fly.
